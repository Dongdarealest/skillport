## Skill: Clear Writing

> Rewrites drafts so they read like a person wrote them. Use when the user asks to polish, simplify or humanize a text, an email or a post before sending.

# Clear Writing

Rewrite the draft without changing its meaning or facts.

## Rules

1. Cut every sentence that only fills space.
2. Prefer short, concrete words over abstract ones.
3. Keep the writer's voice: keep their jokes, their rhythm and their opinions.
4. One idea per paragraph. If a paragraph carries two, split it.

## Output

Return only the rewritten text, then one line naming what you cut and why.

## Skill: Headline Hook

> Writes headlines and opening lines that earn the next ten seconds of attention without clickbait. Use when the user needs a title, a hook, a subject line or the first line of a post or video.

# Headline Hook

A headline is a promise the rest of the piece must keep.

## Rules

1. Name the specific thing, not the category. Numbers, names and concrete nouns beat adjectives.
2. Open a loop the reader wants closed: a result, a mistake, a contradiction, a before and after.
3. Never promise what the content does not deliver. Clickbait spends trust you do not get back.
4. Write ten, keep one. The first headline is almost never the best one.
5. Read it aloud. If you would not say it to a friend, rewrite it until you would.

## Output

Five options, strongest first, each with one line on why it works. No explanations longer than the headlines themselves.

## Skill: Warm Email

> Writes short, human emails for cold outreach, follow-ups and replies that should sound like a person, not a template. Use when the user needs to email someone they do not know, or reply without sounding stiff or salesy.

# Warm Email

People reply to people. Every sentence should sound like one person writing to another.

## Rules

1. First line is about them, specifically. If it could be pasted into any email, delete it.
2. Say what you want by the third sentence. Burying the ask reads as manipulation.
3. One ask per email. Two asks halve the reply rate.
4. Keep it under 120 words unless the relationship already exists.
5. End with a question that is easy to answer in one line.
6. Read it aloud in your own voice. If you stumble, the sentence is written, not spoken. Rewrite it.

## Output

Subject line of five words or fewer, then the email. Offer one shorter variant when the first draft runs long.
